# Lions Tried to Eat a Pangolin. It Didn't End How You Think

Setting: the Kalahari, southern Africa. Hero: a female ground pangolin with her baby riding on her tail.
Format: every beat is a SHOT (what the camera shows, real documentary style) followed by the NARRATION line. About 14–15 minutes.
Camera key: TELE = long telephoto from a hidden position · GROUND = low remote camera at pangolin height · MACRO = extreme close-up · SLOW = 120 fps slow motion · DRONE = aerial · MOONLIT = cinematic moonlit night footage · LOW-LIGHT = night camera.

---

## COLD OPEN — BLOCKBUSTER (0:00–1:00)

SHOT: Black. Silence. MACRO, low-light: one dark eye opens between overlapping scales.
NARRATION: She has no claws to fight with. No teeth. No speed.

SHOT: Deep drum hit. MOONLIT wide: seven lions closing in a ring around one small armoured ball, eyes glinting.
NARRATION: Tonight, seven lions want what is inside her.

SHOT: Music builds. SLOW, LOW-LIGHT TELE: a lioness bites down; her teeth skid off the scales in a spray of sand.
NARRATION: The strongest jaws in Africa…

SHOT: MACRO: a scale edge clamps shut on a lion's claw; the lion flinches back.
NARRATION: …cannot get in.

SHOT: Hard cuts on the beat — MACRO termites swarming her face / SLOW leopard leaping with the ball in its jaws / the ball dropping from a branch / a jackal recoiling, gagging.
NARRATION: Ants. A leopard. Jackals.

SHOT: Music swells. MOONLIT: four hyenas tearing at the ball. SLOW MACRO: a scale cracks.
NARRATION: Hyenas that crush bone. And for the first time, her armour breaks.

SHOT: Total silence. MOONLIT MACRO: through a hairline crack in the scales, a tiny eye glints.
NARRATION: Because she is not protecting herself.

SHOT: Heartbeat sound. LOW-LIGHT TELE: a lion carries the ball in its mouth into the dark.
NARRATION: She is protecting the only thing she has. And she will not open. Not for anything.

SHOT: Music explodes. Fastest cuts — three lions pounce at once / two young males fight over her / the ball rolls into a dry riverbed / a huge paw slams down.
NARRATION: Five hunters. One night. One mother who refuses to break.

SHOT: Sudden stop. One drum hit. DRONE, moonlit: the pride in a ring around the motionless ball on red sand.
NARRATION: Lions can kill a buffalo. They can bring down a giraffe.

SHOT: Slow push-in on the ball under the moon.
NARRATION: So how does an animal the size of a small dog survive the night?

SHOT: Title slam over black: LIONS TRIED TO EAT A PANGOLIN. Cut to golden late afternoon: the pangolin walking upright on her hind legs, a baby riding her tail.
NARRATION: It begins at sunset. Four fights earlier.

## FIGHT 1 — THE ANT ARMY (late afternoon)

SHOT: TELE, heat haze: she walks upright on two legs across red Kalahari sand, tail held out behind her, the baby clinging to it.
NARRATION: She walks on two legs, like a tiny dinosaur. Her tail is a balance pole, and a seat for her baby.

SHOT: GROUND: she stops at a tall termite mound and sniffs along its base.
NARRATION: She is starving. And she has found a fortress full of food.

SHOT: GROUND, SLOW: her long front claws rip into the hard clay; chunks fly toward the lens.
NARRATION: Those claws are not for fighting. They are for breaking into places like this.

SHOT: MACRO: soldier termites pour out of the breach, jaws open, swarming over her snout and eyelids.
NARRATION: And the fortress fights back. Thousands of soldiers, every one of them biting.

SHOT: MACRO: her nostrils pinch shut; her ears fold closed; thick eyelids squeeze tight.
NARRATION: She seals her nose. She seals her ears. She shuts her eyes. And she keeps eating.

SHOT: MACRO, SLOW: a long sticky tongue whips deep into the tunnels and out, coated in termites.
NARRATION: Her tongue is anchored deep inside her chest. It reaches where no claw can.

SHOT: MACRO: soldiers crawl up the baby's scales toward its face. It squeaks.
NARRATION: Then they find the baby.

SHOT: TELE: she spins, shakes her whole body hard, scraping the baby against her flank to knock the soldiers off.
NARRATION: She shakes. She scrapes. Bitten everywhere, she still clears every soldier off her baby first.

SHOT: GROUND: she backs away from the mound, termites still clinging to her face.
NARRATION: She has no teeth. She swallows her food whole, and grinds it in her stomach with sand and stones.

SHOT: Pull focus: fifty metres away, a leopard's head lifts in an acacia, ears locked on the squeak.
NARRATION: But a baby's cry carries. And something in the tree heard it.

---

## FIGHT 2 — THE LEOPARD (sunset)

SHOT: DRONE: a silhouetted acacia against a burning sky; the leopard drops silently to the ground.
NARRATION: A young leopard. Hungry. Curious. And very, very fast.

SHOT: TELE tracking: the leopard stalks low through the grass; in the background, the pangolin walks on, unaware.
NARRATION: She never looks behind her. Her eyesight is poor. She lives by smell, and the wind is wrong.

SHOT: SLOW: the pounce. In the same instant, she drops and curls around the baby into a perfect ball.
NARRATION: It pounces. And in less than a second, she is gone. In her place, a ball of armour.

SHOT: GROUND: the leopard bats the ball; it rolls; the leopard bats it again, ears flat.
NARRATION: Every scale overlaps the next, like tiles on a roof. There is no edge to hold.

SHOT: TELE, SLOW: the leopard bites hard; its teeth skid off the scales.
NARRATION: Her scales are made of keratin, the same material as your fingernails. Layer on layer of it.

SHOT: TELE: the leopard grips the ball in its jaws and carries it up the acacia trunk.
NARRATION: So it does what leopards do with prey. It takes her up the tree.

SHOT: Close-up on the branch: the leopard pins the ball with a paw and hooks a claw under one scale edge.
NARRATION: On the branch, it finds a gap. One claw. Under one scale. Pulling.

SHOT: MACRO: the scale clamps down; the leopard jerks its paw back and shakes it.
NARRATION: She clamps. The scale edges are sharp, and her muscles lock them like a vice.

SHOT: SLOW: the ball slips from the leopard's jaws and drops three metres, hitting the sand.
NARRATION: The leopard loses its grip. She falls. And she does not open.

SHOT: GROUND: the ball bounces and rolls down the side of a dune, faster and faster, into the mouth of a burrow.
NARRATION: She rolls downhill, straight into someone else's home.

---

## FIGHT 3 — THE JACKALS (dusk)

SHOT: TELE: two black-backed jackals and three half-grown pups burst out of the den, ears forward, at the strange ball by their entrance.
NARRATION: Black-backed jackals. A family. And she has landed on their doorstep.

SHOT: GROUND: the pups circle, nipping at the scale edges, yelping.
NARRATION: The pups test her first. Nip. Jump back. Nip again.

SHOT: TELE: an adult jackal grips the ball by an edge and drags it across the sand, away from the den.
NARRATION: The adults are not playing. They drag her into the open.

SHOT: MACRO: the scales grind against each other as she locks tighter.
NARRATION: Every pull makes her lock harder.

SHOT: GROUND, low-light: a second adult flips the ball and pushes its snout into the curl, searching for the soft belly.
NARRATION: Then one of them finds the place where her tail meets her head. The only seam in the armour.

SHOT: Close-up: she releases her musk; the jackal jerks its head back, gagging, rubbing its face in the sand.
NARRATION: And she answers with the one weapon she has. A foul musk, sprayed straight into its face.

SHOT: TELE: the whole family backs off, sneezing, pups shaking their heads.
NARRATION: Jackals eat almost anything. Not this.

SHOT: DRONE, blue hour: the yelping family scatters across the plain; the sound carries far.
NARRATION: But their yelping carries across the Kalahari. And it reaches the worst listeners on the plain.

SHOT: Cut: far away, in the dusk, four hyena heads turn toward the sound.
NARRATION: Hyenas.

---

## FIGHT 4 — THE HYENAS (early night)

SHOT: MOONLIT wide: four hyenas loping in across the silver plain, eyes glinting.
NARRATION: Spotted hyenas. Their jaws can crack the bones of a giraffe.

SHOT: LOW-LIGHT TELE: the first hyena reaches the ball and crunches down; jaw muscles bulge.
NARRATION: No hunter on the plain bites harder.

SHOT: GROUND: two hyenas grip the ball from opposite sides and tug, dragging it through the sand like a toy.
NARRATION: They pull her two ways at once. They roll her. They crush her again.

SHOT: SLOW, LOW-LIGHT: a hyena lifts the ball off the ground in its jaws and shakes it hard.
NARRATION: She is being shaken in a mouth that could break her spine. She stays locked.

SHOT: MACRO, SLOW: under the hyena's teeth, the edge of one scale splits.
NARRATION: And then, a crack.

SHOT: MOONLIT MACRO: through the crack, the baby's tiny face and glinting eye are visible.
NARRATION: For the first time tonight, there is a way in. And her baby is right behind it.

SHOT: TELE: she contracts her whole body; the gap closes; the hyena's teeth slip off.
NARRATION: She pulls tighter than she has ever pulled. The gap closes.

SHOT: GROUND: two hyenas start fighting each other over the ball, snapping and giggling.
NARRATION: Now the hyenas fight each other for her.

SHOT: MOONLIT wide: the giggling rolls out into the dark. Far away, a ring of large heads lifts from the sand.
NARRATION: And that noise is a dinner bell. For lions.

---

## FIGHT 5 — THE LION PRIDE (night) — THE FINAL BATTLE

SHOT: MOONLIT wide: the lions charge in; the hyenas scatter in every direction.
NARRATION: Seven lions. The hyenas do not argue.

SHOT: LOW-LIGHT TELE: a lioness reaches the ball first and pins it under one huge paw.
NARRATION: A lioness claims her first.

SHOT: SLOW: the lioness bites down hard; her teeth slide off; she bites again from another angle.
NARRATION: She bites. Again. From the side. From above. Every bite slides off.

SHOT: TELE: a young male snatches the ball out from under her and runs; two others chase him.
NARRATION: A young male steals her. Two more chase him. Now the lions are fighting over a meal they cannot eat.

SHOT: GROUND: the ball drops and rolls toward a dry riverbed; three lions pounce on it at once.
NARRATION: She rolls toward the riverbed. Three lions land on her at once.

SHOT: MACRO: a lion claw hooks under the cracked scale and pulls.
NARRATION: One of them finds the crack the hyena made.

SHOT: SLOW MACRO: the scale lifts a few millimetres; inside, the baby's tail tip trembles.
NARRATION: The scale lifts. Just a little. Her baby is a claw's width from the night.

SHOT: MACRO: she clamps; the scale snaps down on the claw; the lion yanks back, shaking its paw.
NARRATION: She clamps down on the claw itself. The lion pulls away, shaking its paw.

SHOT: LOW-LIGHT TELE: the big male picks the ball up in his mouth and walks off with it, the pride following.
NARRATION: The pride male takes her. He carries her through the dark, the way a cat carries a toy.

SHOT: MOONLIT wide: minutes pass; he drops her, licks her, rolls her, picks her up again.
NARRATION: He licks her. He rolls her. He carries her again. Lions have been filmed doing this for hours.

SHOT: GROUND: she lies still in the sand; lion paws everywhere around the lens.
NARRATION: She does not move. She does not open. She just waits.

SHOT: MOONLIT wide: one by one, the lions lie down around the ball, losing interest.
NARRATION: And slowly, the strongest hunters in Africa start to lose interest.

SHOT: DRONE, moonlit: the pride asleep in a loose ring around the motionless ball.
NARRATION: Seven lions. Asleep around a meal they could never open.

---

## ENDING (pre-dawn)

SHOT: LOW-LIGHT GROUND: in the grey before dawn, the ball very slowly begins to unroll.
NARRATION: Hours later, in the grey before dawn, she moves.

SHOT: MACRO: the baby's face appears from the curl, blinking, unhurt.
NARRATION: Her baby is there. Not a scratch.

SHOT: TELE: she stands up on her hind legs, the baby climbs onto her tail, and she walks right past a sleeping lioness's nose.
NARRATION: She stands up on two legs, and walks out between the lions, close enough to touch them.

SHOT: TELE: the cracked scale glints on her back as she walks away into the first light.
NARRATION: Five hunters. One cracked scale. And she never opened. Not once.

SHOT: DRONE: the sun rising over the Kalahari, a tiny figure crossing the red sand.
NARRATION: Lions, leopards and hyenas could not open her. But people can. The pangolin is the most trafficked wild mammal on Earth, hunted for its scales.

SHOT: TELE: she disappears into the grass, the baby's tail swinging.
NARRATION: Tonight, she will walk again. And the hunters will be waiting. But she has never needed to fight. She only needs to hold on.

SHOT: Black. One final hit.
NARRATION: If she held your attention, hit subscribe. There are more battles like this coming.
