How does a six-tonne killer whale hunt on land? And why would a mother risk dying on the beach to teach it?

On one stretch of coast in Patagonia, Argentina, killer whales do something almost no other whales on Earth will do. They charge out of the sea and onto the stones to catch sea lion pups, then wriggle back before the waves leave them stranded. This is the story of Marea, a mother orca, and Nico, the son she must teach before the season ends, past a sea lion bull who guards the beach, a falling tide and a storm that could kill them both.

🐋 In this documentary:

• A young orca stranded and fighting to get back to the sea
• A sea lion bull who bites back
• A four-tonne elephant seal bull crashing down on a calf
• A mother stranded as the tide falls, and a storm-wave hunt for the ages

🌍 Only a handful of killer whales on Earth hunt by stranding themselves on purpose, and almost all of them live along this one coast. The skill survives only because mothers teach it to their calves. Protecting Patagonia's coast protects one of the rarest hunting traditions in the natural world.
