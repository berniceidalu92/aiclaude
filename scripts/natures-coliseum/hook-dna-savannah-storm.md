## Structure
1. **Cold open (0:00–1:00):** the hunter, the target, the bond. No explanation.
2. **Chase (1:00–2:15):** the mother drives the hunter off.
3. **Standoff (2:15–3:06):** the mother wanders and the hunter waits.
4. **New threat (3:06–4:54):** the jackals test the mother again and again.
5. **More pressure (4:54–5:43):** more hungry animals join, and the mother is squeezed.
6. **Separation (5:43–7:13):** two attacks, the foal lost in the grass, silence.
7. **Fury (7:13–8:35):** the mother attacks the hunter.
8. **Reunion (8:35–9:45):** the foal is found, then the scars, then home.
9. **Fact section (9:45 onward):** setting reveal, then 5-second facts for each species, linked back to their story roles.

## Rules worth copying (style, never wording)
1. Cast the animals as metaphor roles (Hunger, the Storm, the Thieves), not names, and reveal the real species at the end.
2. Short narration: one line of 5–15 words, then let the footage play for several seconds. Aim for about 1–1.3 words per second in the story section.
3. Every line is one of: a personified abstraction, a rule of the wild, a contrast pair, a twist ("But…"), a callback, or a cliffhanger.
4. Plant 3–4 motifs early (thread, walls and doors, ocean, home) and pay each one off later.
5. Escalate threats: one hunter, then thieves, then a crowd. Each victory opens a new danger.
6. Emotional curve: playful, then chased, then saved, then pressured, then lost and silent, then fury, then reunion.
7. Reveal the setting and species after the story ends, then hand off to a quick fact section that links each species to its story role.
8. Keep the fact section punchy: one fact per line, about 5 seconds each. It is optional, and it is where retention may dip.

## The title
**"Striped Hyena Hunts a Donkey Foal, Unaware of What's Charging Behind Him"** (71 characters)

Why it works:
- **Plain, searchable names first:** "Striped Hyena", "Donkey Foal". The metaphors stay inside the video; the title is literal.
- **A present-tense action:** "Hunts". Viewers feel they are watching it happen.
- **Dramatic irony:** "Unaware of…". The viewer knows something the hunter doesn't, and wants to see it land.
- **A hidden threat:** "What's Charging Behind Him" never says it's the mother. The click is to find out who.
- **Who to root for:** the underdog (the foal) is in danger, and a protector is coming.

The formula: **[Predator] [Hunts/Stalks/Traps] [Prey], Unaware of [Hidden Danger/Twist]**
Variants: "…But Didn't Expect…", "…Then [Protector] Arrives", "…Until…".
