## Description (paste into YouTube)

Her blood is blue. She has three hearts. Most of her nerve cells are in her arms, which can taste, touch and decide on their own. And when she becomes a mother, she stops eating forever.

Follow one giant Pacific octopus through the last year of her life in the cold kelp forests of British Columbia. Watch her turn into a rock in less than a second, escape a sea lion with an ink decoy, and squeeze through a gap the width of a coin. Then follow her into a dark den, where she guards tens of thousands of eggs for six months without a single meal, and gives her very last breath to set her babies free.

Along the way: why octopuses are colour-blind yet perfect at camouflage, the octopus that carries coconut shells, the deep-sea mother who guarded her eggs for four and a half years, and the tiny gland that acts like a self-destruct switch.
