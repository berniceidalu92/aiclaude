# WILD INDIA (Peacock) — Visual Analysis (State 7)

One breeding year in the life of a peacock in a tiger forest, framed by Darwin's 1860 puzzle. It uses the Nature's Coliseum real-documentary look, at its most colourful: the peacock's train is the star of the film.

## Visual style profile (locked for all prompts)
- **Style:** photorealistic landmark wildlife documentary, 16:9 widescreen. It should look like real footage filmed by a natural-history crew from hidden positions, never CGI, fantasy or a staged render.
- **Palette:**
  - **Dry season:** sal forest greens, dry golden meadow grass, red-brown earth and grey rock.
  - **Monsoon:** saturated wet greens.
  - **The peacock:** electric blue neck and iridescent green-bronze-gold train. The colour must look real and shift with the angle of light, never painted or glowing.
  - **The peahens:** soft brown-grey with green necks.
  - **The tiger:** a warm orange accent.
  - **Grade:** natural and rich, never oversaturated.
- **Lighting:**
  - soft dawn and late-afternoon sun for the dry season,
  - grey, diffused monsoon light, which makes iridescence glow,
  - shafts of light through the sal canopy,
  - backlight through the raised train, showing the eyespots.
- **Camera:**
  - **Long range:** long telephoto (400–800mm) for the forest, the meadows and the tiger stalk.
  - **Close range:** remote cameras for feathers, eyespots, spurs and chicks.
  - **Movement:** gimbal tracking for walking and fights, and aerials over the forest and hills.
  - **Slow motion:** true high-frame-rate slow motion for take-offs, leaps, the tiger's charge and the shaking train.
- **Composition:** the full fan always framed completely when it is raised, with no cropped feather tips. Peahens in the foreground watching the display, to show who decides. Tiger shots keep the peacock small in the frame, to show the danger.
- **Detail:** ultra-fine detail on each eyespot, the barbs of every feather, neck scales, leg scales and spurs, rain droplets and dust.
- **Mood:** wonder and beauty, with a constant thread of danger.

## Shot range (mix it to suit the moment)
- **Long range:** the forest and hills, the meadows, the display ground, the tiger stalking from far away.
- **Mid range:** feeding, walking, the display seen in full, the rival fight, the peahen walking around him.
- **Close range:** eyespots and feather structure, the shaking train, the peahen's crest, the call, the spurs, the tiger's eyes, the chicks hatching, feathers falling.

## Darwin scenes (no people)
- The 1860 and 1871 moments are shown only through objects:
  - a Victorian wooden desk lit by an oil lamp, with a handwritten letter, a quill and an inkwell,
  - a single peacock feather lying across the paper,
  - an old leather-bound book.
- No person, hands or face is ever shown. The style is a warm, slightly desaturated still life with candle and oil-lamp light and dust in the air.

## Seasons and lighting
- **Dry season (opening, the roost, the feeding):** golden grass, dusty light, pale dawn.
- **Monsoon (display, fight, choosing):** dark rain clouds, wet leaves, puddles, soft grey light, bright wet greens.
- **Late monsoon (tiger, nest, chicks):** lush green grass, late-afternoon sun breaking through clouds.
- **End of monsoon (shedding the train):** fresh green forest, clear light, fallen feathers in the grass.
- **Night (ending):** moonlight in the roost tree, the tiger in shadow below.

## Character locks (repeat word for word in every prompt)
- **Raja:** an adult male Indian peacock with a shimmering electric-blue neck and breast, a fan-shaped crest, and a long train of iridescent green and bronze feathers covered in eyespots. After the monsoon, he has no train and his short brown tail shows.
- **The young rival:** a younger male Indian peacock with a blue neck and a shorter, thinner train with fewer eyespots.
- **Meera:** a female Indian peahen with mottled brown-grey feathers, a metallic green neck, a white face patch and a small fan-shaped crest.
- **The peahens:** a small group of brown-grey Indian peahens with green necks.
- **The peachicks:** tiny fluffy Indian peafowl chicks with soft brown and cream down and small sprouting wing feathers.
- **The tigress:** an adult Bengal tigress with a bright orange coat, bold black stripes and amber eyes.
- **Supporting animals:** grey langur monkeys with black faces, spotted deer (chital).

## Locations
1. **The roost tree:** a tall sal or arjun tree at the forest edge, with high bare branches against the sky.
2. **The meadow:** an open grassy meadow ringed by sal forest and rocky hills.
3. **The display ground:** a small patch of bare red-brown earth under a large mahua tree at a forest clearing.
4. **The forest edge:** long grass at the edge of the sal forest, where the tigress waits.
5. **The nest:** a shallow scrape in thick grass under a bush.
6. **Darwin's study:** a Victorian desk with lamp, letter, quill and feather.
7. **Background:** Bandhavgarh in central India, with sal forest, rocky hills, ancient cliffs and meadows.

## Continuity between beats
- Each scene keeps one season, time of day and lighting from first beat to last. Each beat uses the previous beat's image as its reference.
- Raja's train carries forward: full and perfect through the season, then a few feathers missing after the tiger's claws, then shed completely at the end.
- The chicks grow from just hatched, to following Meera, to fluttering into branches.

## Rules
- Every prompt stands alone, with the full character description, location, season and style.
- Every beat has a plain-language Description. Video prompts carry a stability line so animals keep the same body, feather pattern and number of legs, with no morphing.
- Correct anatomy: peafowl have two legs and two wings, with one train on males only. The tiger has four legs and one tail. No extra limbs or duplicated animals.
- No text, logos, music or people (the Darwin scenes are objects only), no villages or vehicles, and no gore.
