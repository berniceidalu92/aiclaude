# A Leopard Hunts a Gorilla Family… Then the Silverback Wakes Up

A silverback charges through the morning mist.

In front of him, a leopard. It has hunted his family all night.

He weighs one hundred and seventy kilos.

The leopard only wants the smallest one.

Since yesterday, he has fought an elephant…

…a gang of twenty-five chimpanzees…

…and a rival who wants to take everything he has.

The Congo rainforest. Here, a newborn gorilla is the easiest meal in the forest.

He is hurt. He is tired.

And the leopard has been waiting for exactly this.

This morning, he has one fight left.

It begins at sunrise. One day earlier.

Deep in the Congo, there are open clearings in the forest, full of water and minerals. Every animal comes here.

This is a silverback. The leader of his family, and its only protector.

Behind him come three females. And the newest member of his family.

The baby is two weeks old. It weighs two kilos, and it can't let go of its mother.

He eats. But he never stops watching the trees.

Then the trees at the edge of the clearing start to shake.

A forest elephant bull. Three tonnes. And he wants the clearing to himself.

He shakes his head and trumpets. A warning.

The females pull back. The silverback doesn't.

He stands and beats his chest. That sound carries for a kilometre through the forest.

The elephant charges, and stops short. A test.

The silverback charges back, slapping the water and roaring.

For a moment, neither of them moves.

Then the elephant charges for real. A tusk catches his shoulder and throws him into the mud.

He gets up. And he stands in front of his family again.

The elephant stops. It has made its point.

The silverback leads his family into the trees. He is limping.

They move deep into the forest, toward the fruit trees.

A fig tree, heavy with ripe fruit. After the clearing, they need to eat.

But someone else came here first.

Chimpanzees. Twenty-five of them. A gang that hunts together.

Then they start to scream.

They drop out of the tree all around the family.

They go straight for the mother with the baby. Scientists have filmed chimpanzee gangs attacking gorilla families like this.

The silverback charges into them. Two chimps are knocked aside.

Three more drop onto his back from the branches.

He rolls, and throws them off.

A chimp grabs the mother. The baby screams.

The silverback is there in two strides. The chimp lets go.

The gang flees back into the trees, screaming.

The baby is safe. But he is bitten and exhausted. And the screaming has carried far.

Someone heard it. A younger silverback who has followed this family for days.

He has no family of his own. He wants this one.

When a new male takes over a family, he often kills the babies that aren't his.

The old silverback stands up. His shoulder is bruised. His hand is bitten.

The rival beats his chest. The old silverback answers.

The rival charges, crashing through the bushes, and swerves away at the last moment.

The second charge isn't a bluff. They slam together.

They roll across the forest floor, biting and grappling.

Their canine teeth are as long as a leopard's.

The rival goes for the hurt shoulder. The old silverback roars.

They break apart. Both of them stand panting, staring.

Then the old silverback charges, with everything he has left, and drives the rival into a tree.

The rival backs away. But he doesn't leave. He sits at the edge of the trees, watching.

The old silverback has won. But he can barely stand.

Night falls over the Congo.

The family builds nests from leaves and branches, on the forest floor.

The mother curls around her baby.

The silverback lies between his family and the dark.

In the undergrowth, two eyes.

A leopard. It has followed the smell of his wounds all evening. Leopards here can kill a gorilla.

It doesn't attack. It waits for him to sleep.

Just before dawn, the silverback falls asleep.

The leopard comes down from the tree.

It creeps toward the mother's nest. Toward the baby.

The mother wakes, and screams.

The silverback is awake before the scream ends.

The leopard turns to face him. It snarls and swipes.

He swings his arm. The leopard leaps up a tree.

It crouches on a branch above him. Waiting.

Then it drops onto his back.

Claws dig into his shoulders. He spins and crashes to the ground.

He reaches back, grabs it, and throws it into the bushes.

He charges after it, roaring. And this time, the leopard runs.

He stands in the mist and beats his chest one last time.

The baby is safe. Not a scratch.

An elephant. Twenty-five chimpanzees. A rival. A leopard. One day and one night, and he never stepped back.

His family gathers around him.

But at the edge of the forest, the rival is still watching. And he is younger.
