## Title
The Great Ibex Circle and Why They Don't Live on the Ground

## Description (paste into YouTube)

A ledge no wider than a hand, three hundred meters above the valley. And standing on it, perfectly still, is a goat.

In the Karakoram mountains of northern Pakistan, an ibex family lives its whole life on the edge of a cliff. Down on the ground there is grass, water and salt, and a snow leopard waiting beside every one of them. This is the great ibex circle: every day down to the ground for what they need, and every day back up to the cliffs to live. Follow one ibex family for a full year: hooves that grip rock like climbing shoes, a secret salt lick, the battles of the rut, a winter at thirty below, two snow leopard hunts, and a kid born on a ledge barely wide enough to lie on, climbing within days.

In parts of the Karakoram, local communities now protect the ibex, and the herds are growing again. But the mountains are warming, and both the ibex and the snow leopard are being squeezed into less and less of their world.
