# 7 Lions Catch a Mother Pangolin… But They Don’t Know What She’s Hiding

She has no claws to fight with. No teeth. No speed.

Tonight, seven lions want what is inside her.

The strongest jaws in Africa…

…cannot get in.

Ants. A leopard. Jackals.

Hyenas that crush bone. And for the first time, her armour breaks.

Because she is not protecting herself.

She is protecting the only thing she has. And she will not open. Not for anything.

Five hunters. One night. One mother who refuses to break.

Lions can kill a buffalo. They can bring down a giraffe.

So how does an animal the size of a small dog survive the night?

It begins at sunset. Four fights earlier.

She walks on two legs, like a tiny dinosaur. Her tail is a balance pole, and a seat for her baby.

She is starving. And she has found a fortress full of food.

Those claws are not for fighting. They are for breaking into places like this.

And the fortress fights back. Thousands of soldiers, every one of them biting.

She seals her nose. She seals her ears. She shuts her eyes. And she keeps eating.

Her tongue is anchored deep inside her chest. It reaches where no claw can.

Then they find the baby.

She shakes. She scrapes. Bitten everywhere, she still clears every soldier off her baby first.

She has no teeth. She swallows her food whole, and grinds it in her stomach with sand and stones.

But a baby's cry carries. And something in the tree heard it.

A young leopard. Hungry. Curious. And very, very fast.

She never looks behind her. Her eyesight is poor. She lives by smell, and the wind is wrong.

It pounces. And in less than a second, she is gone. In her place, a ball of armour.

Every scale overlaps the next, like tiles on a roof. There is no edge to hold.

Her scales are made of keratin, the same material as your fingernails. Layer on layer of it.

So it does what leopards do with prey. It takes her up the tree.

On the branch, it finds a gap. One claw. Under one scale. Pulling.

She clamps. The scale edges are sharp, and her muscles lock them like a vice.

The leopard loses its grip. She falls. And she does not open.

She rolls downhill, straight into someone else's home.

Black-backed jackals. A family. And she has landed on their doorstep.

The pups test her first. Nip. Jump back. Nip again.

The adults are not playing. They drag her into the open.

Every pull makes her lock harder.

Then one of them finds the place where her tail meets her head. The only seam in the armour.

And she answers with the one weapon she has. A foul musk, sprayed straight into its face.

Jackals eat almost anything. Not this.

But their yelping carries across the Kalahari. And it reaches the worst listeners on the plain.

Hyenas.

Spotted hyenas. Their jaws can crack the bones of a giraffe.

No hunter on the plain bites harder.

They pull her two ways at once. They roll her. They crush her again.

She is being shaken in a mouth that could break her spine. She stays locked.

And then, a crack.

For the first time tonight, there is a way in. And her baby is right behind it.

She pulls tighter than she has ever pulled. The gap closes.

Now the hyenas fight each other for her.

And that noise is a dinner bell. For lions.

Seven lions. The hyenas do not argue.

A lioness claims her first.

She bites. Again. From the side. From above. Every bite slides off.

A young male steals her. Two more chase him. Now the lions are fighting over a meal they cannot eat.

She rolls toward the riverbed. Three lions land on her at once.

One of them finds the crack the hyena made.

The scale lifts. Just a little. Her baby is a claw's width from the night.

She clamps down on the claw itself. The lion pulls away, shaking its paw.

The pride male takes her. He carries her through the dark, the way a cat carries a toy.

He licks her. He rolls her. He carries her again. Lions have been filmed doing this for hours.

She does not move. She does not open. She just waits.

And slowly, the strongest hunters in Africa start to lose interest.

Seven lions. Asleep around a meal they could never open.

Hours later, in the grey before dawn, she moves.

Her baby is there. Not a scratch.

She stands up on two legs, and walks out between the lions, close enough to touch them.

Five hunters. One cracked scale. And she never opened. Not once.

Lions, leopards and hyenas could not open her. But people can. The pangolin is the most trafficked wild mammal on Earth, hunted for its scales.

Tonight, she will walk again. And the hunters will be waiting. But she has never needed to fight. She only needs to hold on.
