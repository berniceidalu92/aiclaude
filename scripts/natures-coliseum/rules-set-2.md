# Nature's Coliseum — Rule Set 2 (active)

> **Status: active.** These are the rules for all new videos from now on.
> Rule Set 1 (the old standing rules, rules 1–21) is saved in `hook-dna-desert-rules.md` and not active. Any of its rules can be brought back when needed by asking for it by name or number.

Source: forensic breakdown of a viral reference video (an old lion and his white-lion son defending a kopje from two young brothers, about 12:50). Copy the style, never the wording.

## Rules

### Hook (0:00–2:00)
1. **Open mid-problem with a weakness, not a fight.** A continuous real scene, not a montage teaser. Show a physical struggle we can see (slipping, stuck, failing), then the hero helps or protects.
2. **Deliver the thumbnail within 20 seconds.** The animal and situation on the thumbnail are on screen by 0:20.
3. **Hold back one identity, then reveal it within 90 seconds** with one flat, short sentence (e.g. "The old lion is his father.").
4. **Open several questions in the first 30 seconds** (who is this, why is it helping, what's wrong) and close at least one before 2:00.

### Hero and story
5. **The hero is a bond, not a single animal:** mother and baby, father and son, siblings. One protects, one can't protect itself.
6. **The calm section is a list of weaknesses.** Every detail shown (worn teeth, a limp, cloudy eyes, rasping breath) must come back later in the story. Nothing is wasted.
7. **Bring the threat in slowly with an insult** (scent marking, stealing, staring, covering a mark) before any violence. Then state the stakes in one line and release: "Then they come."
8. **One antagonist** (a single animal or one team), not a chain of different enemies.

### The fight
9. **One long fight (about 40–50% of the video) in 5–7 phases.** Each phase changes who is winning: win → losing → down on one knee → pause → twist → trapped → turnaround → victory.
10. **A stillness beat in the middle of the fight:** everyone stops, dust settles, heavy breathing. Contrast makes the next strike land.
11. **A rule-breaking twist at about two-thirds of the way through:** the attacker ignores the hero and goes for the weak one. The stakes move to the one who can't defend itself.
12. **The weak one saves the strong one at the climax.** This pays off the weakness set up in the hook (mirror structure, roles reversed).

### Ending
13. **Bittersweet and realistic:** the hero wins the fight but loses something (territory, health, time). No fairy-tale ending.
14. **Emotional bookend:** the last image echoes the first (the opening push mirrored by a head laid on a paw).
15. **Open threat at the end:** the enemy is still out there, watching. This sets up a sequel and subscriptions.

### Retention
16. **A small rehook every 30–60 seconds, a big one at every chapter boundary.** Use reveals, pattern interrupts (new characters, sudden silence), status lines ("extraordinarily rare"), death clocks ("few live this long") and stakes lines.
17. **Escalate emotional cost, not just violence:** territory → the hero's life → the weak one's life → the weak one saving the hero.

### Writing style
18. **Plain, filmable language.** Present tense, sentences of 8–12 words, physical verbs. No metaphors, no nicknames for animals, no hype lines from the narrator.
19. **Never name emotions.** Show them through action ("He does not move.").
20. **Short one-line punches after long passages** ("Then they come." "The old lion rises.") and repetition for rhythm ("He gives up a step, then another.").
21. **Facts: two sentences at most, placed right before they matter**, always explaining what's on screen. About 90% visible action, 10% fact.
