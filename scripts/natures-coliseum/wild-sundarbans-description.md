Twice a day, this forest disappears. And the tigers who live here have learned to swim with the killers underneath.

In the Sundarbans, the largest mangrove forest on Earth, a tigress raises two cubs where the tide swallows the land twice a day. Saltwater crocodiles wait in the channels. King cobras hunt in the roots. Bull sharks follow the tide upriver. Then a rival tigress claims her island, and a cyclone tears her family apart in the dark.

So how do you raise a family in a forest that drowns twice a day?

🐅 In this documentary:

• Mother vs rival: two tigresses battle for an island and a cub

🌍 The sea in the Sundarbans is rising faster than the global average, and cyclones are growing stronger. The mangroves protect millions of people from the storms, and the tigers protect the forest. There is hope: tiger numbers here have held steady and recently even risen.
