# 5 Wolves Corner a Wolverine… They Have No Idea What She’s Fighting For

Five wolves surround a wolverine in the snow.

The biggest one lunges. She meets it with her teeth.

Two more come from behind. She spins to face them.

She weighs twelve kilos. Each wolf weighs four times more.

This morning, she fought a lynx for food.

At noon, an eagle came for her from the sky.

And when the sun goes down, something much bigger is coming.

She has crossed thirty kilometres of snow today.

Because behind her, two kits are waiting in the snow.

She hasn't eaten in three days. Neither have they.

She doesn't back down. Not from wolves. Not from a bear.

It begins at dawn. Eight hours earlier.

This is a wolverine. The largest weasel on land, and one of the toughest animals in the north.

Under the snow, she has dug a den. Her two kits are six weeks old.

They can't hunt. They can't even leave the den. Everything they eat, she has to bring back.

Her wide feet work like snowshoes. Where a wolf sinks, she runs.

The Yukon, Canada. Late March. The hungriest weeks of the year, when food is almost gone.

Then she catches a smell. Fresh blood, somewhere in the trees.

A lynx has just killed a snowshoe hare. It's the same size as she is.

She doesn't hide. She doesn't wait. She walks straight in.

The lynx flattens its ears and hisses. This is its kill.

She charges. The lynx rears up and slashes at her face.

They crash together and roll through the snow, biting and clawing.

The lynx's claws rake her shoulder. Her thick fur takes most of it.

She grabs its back leg. The lynx yowls and twists around.

It tears free and leaps up a spruce tree.

She goes up after it. Wolverines climb too.

The lynx jumps from the branch and runs. It wants no more of her.

The first meal of the day is hers. Now she has to get it home.

To reach the den, she has to cross open snow. There's nowhere to hide.

High above, a golden eagle has seen her.

It folds its wings and dives.

Its talons hit her back. She rolls and snaps at its legs.

The eagle lifts off. She stands over the hare and snarls at the sky.

It comes again from behind, wings beating around her head. She bites off a mouthful of feathers.

On the third dive, it tears the hare out of her jaws.

She chases it. But she can't chase an eagle into the sky.

A whole morning of fighting, and she has nothing to take home.

Then she hears ravens.

Ravens gather over meat. Hunters all across the north follow them to it.

She runs for six kilometres without stopping.

A moose. Killed by wolves last night. And the wolves are gone.

She eats fast. Her jaws can crush meat frozen as hard as wood.

Then she marks it with her musk. As far as she's concerned, it's hers now.

But the wolves only left to sleep. And now they're back.

Five wolves. The pack that killed this moose.

Any other animal her size would run. She stays.

The lead wolf rushes in. She bites its nose, and it jumps back yelping.

The wolves start to circle. They're trying to get behind her.

One darts in from behind. She spins and snaps, and it backs off.

Another grabs the moose and pulls. She jumps straight at its face.

One wolf could kill her. But none of them wants to be the one she bites.

For a moment, everyone stops. Their breath steams in the cold air.

Then two come at once, from both sides. She's knocked into the snow.

A wolf grabs her by the neck and lifts her. She twists, and bites deep into its leg.

It drops her. She climbs straight back onto the moose.

Now three come together. This time she grabs a mouthful of meat and runs.

Jaws snap shut just below her feet. She's up the tree.

The wolves circle below. Above them, she eats.

Wolves can wait for hours. And they won't leave their kill.

At sunset, the wolves stand up, all at once. Their fur rises.

A grizzly. Just woken from five months asleep, and starving.

It charges. All five wolves scatter.

The moose belongs to the bear now. It weighs twenty times more than she does.

And she climbs down from the tree.

She walks right up to it, and grabs the leg she started on this afternoon.

The bear roars in her face. She roars back.

One swipe of its paw throws her into the snow.

She gets up. She goes straight back in, and bites its paw.

The bear rears up on its back legs. She doesn't move.

It crashes down. She's already gone.

While it turns, she tears the leg free at the joint.

A leg almost as big as she is. And she drags it into the dark.

She drags it for hours, for kilometres. Under the northern lights.

Near midnight, she reaches the snowdrift.

Her kits are there. Both of them. And tonight, they eat.

A lynx. An eagle. Five wolves. A grizzly. She never backed down from any of them.

But down in the valley, the bear has found her trail.

If she earned your respect, hit subscribe. There are more battles like this coming.
