He was bitten in the face by one of Africa's deadliest snakes. He collapsed. Two hours later, he woke up and ate it.

Meet Tau, a honey badger living alone in the red sands of the Kalahari, a desert ruled by lions, leopards and venomous snakes. He is no bigger than a dog. Yet he raids beehives through hundreds of stings, hunts cobras for food, and stands his ground when lions surround him in the dark.

So how does an animal this small survive in a land ruled by giants?

🌍 Honey badgers are often trapped or poisoned by people protecting beehives and livestock. A simple fix is saving them: raising beehives on stands badgers cannot reach. When we make room for fearless animals, they find a way to live alongside us.
